## Executive Summary

Applied Materials is the broadest, #1-positioned semicap-equipment franchise — leader in leading-edge foundry logic, DRAM, and advanced packaging, which management says will drive ~75-80% of WFE growth through 2030, with a target to gain >2 points of share. It is Darwin-quality: ~36% ROCE, 41% ROE, 30% net margin, D/E 0.30, strong FCF, capital return. Q3 FY26 was a record (rev $9.12B +25%, non-GAAP EPS $3.50 +41%) — its **fifth straight beat** — yet the stock is down ~41% from its high and **oversold (RSI 33)**. It is **cheaper than LRCX** (PE ~40 vs 52, PEG 0.91 vs 1.40, forward PE ~26 vs 33). The brakes: it still **fails Graham** (fair value ~$244-289 vs $427), carries $3.8B goodwill, and the **CEO and CFO sold near the $700 highs** (a mild negative signal). Verdict: **BUY-ON-WEAKNESS, start small — the cheapest high-quality way to play the WFE/AI dip, but not a Graham bargain yet.**

## 3. Moat Analysis

- **Breadth leadership**: #1 process-equipment position across leading-edge foundry/logic, DRAM, and advanced packaging — the exact segments driving the AI buildout (75-80% of WFE growth 2025-30).
- **Share-gain story**: targeting +2 points of WFE share by 2030 — offense, not just defense.
- **Materials-engineering franchise + installed base**: broad product line and services (AGS) create recurring revenue and switching costs.
- Durability: **Strong**; broader than LRCX but with slightly lower returns on capital (goodwill + capex intensity).

## 4. Management & Insider Activity (DB)

## 6. Valuation

**Multiples:** PE ~39.7 trailing / ~26 forward | PB ~14 | PS ~12 | **PEG 0.91** — cheaper than LRCX on every axis, and PEG < 1 is notable for this quality.

**Graham IV** (Y = 6.30% AAA):

| EPS basis | Growth g | V = EPS×(8.5+2g)×4.4/Y | MoS vs $427 |
|-----------|----------|------------------------|-------------|
| TTM $10.75 | 12% | **$244** | −75% |
| TTM $10.75 | 15% | $289 | −48% |
| TTM $10.75 | 20% | $357 | −20% |

**Graham verdict: FAIL at $427**, but the gap to fair value is **smaller than LRCX's** (−75% vs −129% at g=12%), and at an aggressive-but-defensible 20% growth it's only −20% — i.e. AMAT is closer to justifiable than LRCX. Still, no classic margin of safety.

**Graham + Darwin synthesis:** `Graham FAIL + Darwin PASS` — acceptable for a quality buyer paying up, but it does not clear the ">25% below Graham IV" bar for quality names. AMAT is the *better-value* of the two.
