## Executive Summary

Starbucks is a strong consumer brand in the middle of a **real, working turnaround** under CEO Brian Niccol ("Back to Starbucks"): comps swung from 7 straight quarters of decline to +6.2% (Q2) and +8.1% North America (Q3), and FY26 guidance was raised. **But Darwin's framework is built to say no to exactly this setup.** It triggers Kill List #5 (turnaround) and #2 (high leverage) hard: shareholder equity is **negative −$7.67B**, net debt/EBITDA is **4.44**, the current ratio is **<1 (0.76)**, and the dividend consumed **142% of net income / 113% of FCF in FY25**. On top of that, insiders — led by the CEO, International — have been **selling repeatedly** through 2026. Earnings are depressed (TTM EPS $1.73, down from $2.33) yet the stock trades at **61x trailing / 36x forward.** Graham IV implies the stock is **~2–3x over fair value.** Verdict: **HOLD only as a turnaround bet you already own; do NOT add. Darwin would not initiate here.**

## 3. Moat Analysis

- **Brand** is the moat — premium pricing power, loyalty program, ubiquity, "third place." Genuinely strong and the reason the turnaround has traction (customers came back when experience improved).
- **Erosion check:** the 7-quarter comp decline showed the moat is *not* impregnable — it was dented by execution (throughput, pricing, experience) and competition (value-focused and local chains, especially China). Niccol is repairing it.
- Durability rating: **Moderate-to-Strong brand, but demonstrably damageable.** Not the "cannot be competed away" moat Darwin prizes.

## 4. Management & Insider Activity (DB)

- **Insider selling cluster (bearish):** CEO, International sold repeatedly — 2026-08-05 (2,229 @ $105.99), 07-06 (2,229 @ $104), 06-11, 06-05, 05-05, 04-17, 04-06; EVP Chief Partner Officer also sold (2026-04-29, 2,000 @ $105). Directors received awards (type A) but the operating-executive flow is one-directional **selling**, mostly near the highs ($100–106) above today's $94.43.
- Capital allocation: paying a dividend the company cannot cover from earnings while equity is negative is the opposite of Darwin's "capital-allocation discipline." It protects the dividend-aristocrat narrative at the expense of deleveraging.

## 5. Financial Fortress — Fails

Negative equity, current ratio <1, 4.44x net debt/EBITDA, dividend > FCF historically. This is a **leveraged** consumer company, not a fortress. In a Darwin sense it fails Pillar 2's "cash cow / low debt" requirement outright.
