Date: 2026-10-15 | Price: $72.82 (post-split) | 52-wk High: $124 | Down: ~41% from high | RSI 56

> Framework: Pulak Prasad "Investing from Darwin" + Benjamin Graham valuation.
> Data: app DB (2026-10-15) + web research. $bn unless noted. Prices post-split.

---

## Executive Summary

Netflix is a high-quality business (ROCE ~39%, ROE 49.5%, 28% net margin, strong FCF, no goodwill, $9.9B buyback) that has suffered a **real ~41% decline** — not a cosmetic dip. Unlike LRCX/AMAT, this drop is fundamentally driven: weak Q3 guidance, the decision to **stop reporting subscriber counts** (which removed Wall Street's favorite growth gauge and forced a valuation reset), rising US cancellations/slowing engagement, and analyst downgrades on margin + FX. The silver lining for a value buyer: after the reset, **NFLX is the first name in this series to actually clear Graham** — intrinsic value ~$73-87 vs a $73 price, i.e. roughly fair-to-slightly-cheap. Verdict: **the most Graham-interesting name reviewed, but the decline reflects a genuine growth-deceleration question. A reasonable starter for a quality buyer; confirm the thesis at the imminent Q3 print before sizing up.**

## 3. Moat Analysis

- **Scale + content flywheel**: largest subscriber base funds the largest content budget, which retains/attracts subscribers. Still the streaming leader.
- **Brand + habit**: default "first app" for streaming; pricing power demonstrated by repeated hikes.
- **Ad tier**: a genuine new growth/margin lever that's working.
- **Erosion risk**: engagement is slowing and US is maturing; the moat is wide but no longer *widening* as fast — which is the crux of the de-rating.
- Durability: **Strong but maturing.**

## 4. Management & Insider Activity (DB)

- Capital allocation: good — $9.9B buyback, no dilution.
- **Insider: bearish signal.** CFO, Co-CEO, and Chief Legal Officer all sold in Aug 2026 at $72-76 — right around today's price. Cluster selling by top executives into weakness is a caution flag on near-term conviction.

## 5. Financial Fortress

FCF/NI 82% (a major positive inflection — NFLX historically burned cash on content, now generates real FCF), low-ish leverage, no goodwill. Current ratio 1.14 is thin but fine for a subscription business with predictable cash. Passes Darwin's cash-cow test now in a way it couldn't years ago.

## 6. Valuation — the surprise

**Multiples:** PE ~25.5 trailing / ~25.7 (DB) — web notes forward PE compressed to ~20 after the reset. PEG 1.81. PB 11.2.

**Graham IV** (Y = 6.30% AAA, TTM EPS $3.23):

| Growth g | V = EPS×(8.5+2g)×4.4/Y | MoS vs $72.82 |

**Graham verdict: PASS (borderline).** This is the **first name in the recent series to clear Graham** — at a conservative 12% growth it's essentially at fair value, and at 15% there's a ~16% margin. The 41% decline did the work: NFLX is no longer priced for perfection.

**Graham + Darwin synthesis:** `Graham PASS + Darwin PASS` (with cautions) → the rare "both agree" quadrant. The catch is that the Graham PASS depends on NFLX sustaining ~12%+ earnings growth — the very thing the market now doubts.
