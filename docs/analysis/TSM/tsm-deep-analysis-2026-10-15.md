## Executive Summary

TSMC is arguably the single highest-quality business in this entire series — a near-monopoly in advanced-node foundry with a **50% net margin**, ~36% ROCE, ~40% ROE, a fortress balance sheet (D/E 0.15), no goodwill, and an irreplaceable position: Apple, NVIDIA, AMD, and Qualcomm all depend on it, and HPC/AI is now 66% of revenue. **It just reported record Q3 FY26 today (Oct 15)**: EPS $4.60 beat $4.45, revenue +41% YoY, sixth straight record quarter, and management *raised* 2026 growth guidance to ~44% on "exceptionally strong, supply-constrained" AI demand. Unlike NFLX, this is a business firing on all cylinders in a confirmed uptrend (dip of −14%, not a decline). The two real brakes: (1) **valuation** — even on strong growth, Graham IV (~$363-420) sits around/below the $417 price, so there's little margin of safety; and (2) **the geopolitical tail risk** — Taiwan/China. Verdict: **best business reviewed, fairly priced, but carries a unique and un-diversifiable geopolitical risk that must be sized for. Buy-on-weakness quality; do not oversize.**

Mitigants: Arizona + (planned) Texas fabs diversify geography; TSM is so irreplaceable that a conflict would be a global-economy event, not just a TSM event. But the risk cannot be fully hedged.

## 3. Moat Analysis

- **Process-technology monopoly**: effectively the only foundry that can manufacture leading-edge (3nm, ramping 2nm) at scale and yield. ASML + TSM + a few customers define the frontier.
- **Scale + capital moat**: $60-64B/yr capex is a barrier no new entrant can match; customers co-invest and design around TSM's process.
- **Switching costs**: redesigning chips for another foundry is multi-year and risky — Apple/NVDA/AMD/QCOM are captive.
- Durability: **Exceptional** — the widest moat in the series. The only thing that breaks it is geopolitics, not competition.

## 4. Management & Insider Activity

- World-class operational and capital-allocation track record; pays a growing dividend.
- No insider data in DB (foreign private issuer — Form 4 doesn't apply the same way). Neutral.

## 5. Financial Fortress

D/E 0.15, 50% net margin, 64% gross margin, net cash. The only "weakness" is FCF/NI 51% — but that's by choice (massive growth capex), not distress. The 2nm ramp will compress gross margin 3-4 points near-term (a known, manageable headwind). 10/10 solvency.

## 6. Valuation

**Multiples:** PE ~30.8 trailing / ~24.6 forward | PEG 0.99 | PB 10.6 | PS 15.4. PEG ~1 is reasonable for 40%+ growth.

**Graham IV** (Y = 6.30% AAA; USD TTM EPS ≈ $13.5 for the ADR):

| Growth g | V = EPS×(8.5+2g)×4.4/Y | MoS vs $417 |
|----------|------------------------|-------------|
| 15% (conservative vs 44% guide) | **$363** | −15% |
| 18% | $420 | ~0% (fair) |
| 20% | $448 | +7% |

**Graham verdict: borderline FAIL / fair** — at a conservative 15% it's ~15% overvalued; at 18% it's fair; only above 18% does a margin open. Given management guided ~44% growth, using 15-18% is deliberately conservative, so TSM is **closer to fair value than LRCX/AMAT** but not a Graham bargain. The stock also just ran up (+13% 6mo), so you're not buying a washout.

**Graham + Darwin synthesis:** `Graham borderline + Darwin PASS (ex-geopolitics)` → a fairly-priced elite business. The decision hinges less on valuation than on **how you weight the geopolitical tail.**

## 10. Position Sizing

- Not currently owned (TSM is tracked in DB but not a position).
- **Suggested approach:** TSM is a legitimate core-quality holding, but the Taiwan tail risk means **size it smaller than its quality alone would suggest** — this is Darwin's Pillar 1 (a single catastrophic loss can't be recovered). A modest position with adds on deeper dips (toward the ~$360 Graham-reasonable level) respects both the quality and the tail. The Arizona/Texas diversification is a slow mitigant to watch.
