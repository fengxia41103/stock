# How to Pick a Good Company

Based on Pulak Prasad, *What I Learned About Investing from Darwin* (2023)
Pulak Prasad — Founder/CEO of Nalanda Capital, ~$5B AUM, focused on listed Indian equities

---

## Core Philosophy

Investing is evolution: **Survive first, maximize second.**

Three pillars:

1. **Avoid big risks** — Not losing money matters more than making money
2. **Buy high quality at a fair price** — Only buy businesses fit to survive
3. **Be very lazy** — After buying, barely move; let compounding work

---

## Pillar 1: Avoid Big Risks

**"In evolution, as in investing, the penalty for fatal mistakes is extinction."**

### Type I Errors (Fatal Mistakes) — Must Avoid

Losing 50% requires gaining 100% just to break even. A single large loss can wipe out years of small gains.

### Kill List (Exclusion Criteria)

If any of the following is triggered → **Reject immediately, do not analyze further:**

| # | Exclusion Criterion | Rationale |
|---|---------------------|-----------|
| 1 | **Dishonest controlling shareholder/management** | Cheated once, will cheat again. Unfixable. |
| 2 | **High debt/leverage** | Amplifies downside; lethal in downturns |
| 3 | **Serial acquirers** | Destroys value far more often than creates it |
| 4 | **Rapidly changing industry** | Today's winner is tomorrow's loser |
| 5 | **Turnaround situations** | Most distressed companies stay distressed |
| 6 | **Misaligned interests** | Management/major shareholders vs. minority |
| 7 | **State-owned enterprises** | Non-market decisions; shareholders come last |
| 8 | **Over-dependence on single customer/product** | Concentration risk; lose it and collapse |
| 9 | **Complex/opaque business model** | What you can't understand, you can't assess |
| 10 | **History of fraud/manipulation** | Culture doesn't change |

### In Practice

- Run the Kill List **before** any analysis
- If any criterion is triggered → Stop. Move on.
- Better to miss a good opportunity than to buy a bad company

## Pillar 2: Buy Quality at a Fair Price

**"In investing, as in Siberia, only the fit survive."**

### What Is "High Quality"?

The core measure of a company's "survival fitness" is **ROCE/ROIC (Return on Capital Employed/Invested):**

```
ROCE = EBIT / Capital Employed
```

**Sustained high ROCE implies the simultaneous presence of:**

#### 1. A Strong Moat

Excess returns not eroded by competition → structural barriers exist.

| Moat Type | How to Identify |
|-----------|----------------|
| Brand | Can it charge a premium? Do consumers refuse substitutes? |
| Network effects | Does value increase with each additional user? |
| Switching costs | How expensive is it for customers to leave? |
| Cost advantage | Scale/process/geographic structural cost leadership? |
| Supply constraint | Capacity/license/resource that cannot be replicated? |

#### 2. Excellent Management

Mediocre management cannot sustain high ROCE long-term. Check:
- Capital allocation discipline (no empire-building, no reckless M&A)
- Integrity track record (do what they say, transparent disclosure)
- Long-term orientation (won't sacrifice long-term value for short-term numbers)
- Aligned incentives (ownership, compensation structure)

#### 3. Financial Strength — Cash Cow

- Abundant free cash flow (FCF ≈ or > Net Income)
- Low or zero debt
- Can grow without external financing
- Dividends + buybacks demonstrate real earnings power

#### 4. Sustainable Growth (Self-Funding)

- Organic growth > acquisition-driven growth
- Ample reinvestment runway (high ROCE + long growth runway = perfect compounding machine)
- Industry structure supports long-term growth

### Don't Overpay for Quality

**"Even the best company becomes a bad investment if bought too expensively."**

- Don't rely on precise DCF models ("Darwin ate my DCF")
- The future is unknowable; don't pretend to forecast 10-year cash flows precisely
- Use **margin of safety** instead of point estimates
- Focus on: PE relative to history, FCF yield, peer comparison
- Buy during market panic / temporary difficulties → good companies mis-priced by Mr. Market

### Don't Confuse a Green Frog for a Guppy

- Surface similarity ≠ underlying quality
- Deep-dive into business model, competitive dynamics, management quality
- Don't be seduced by headline growth rates or narratives
- Triangulate: multiple sources, seek disconfirming evidence, use checklists

## Pillar 3: Be Very Lazy

**"Sometimes, the best investment decision is to do nothing."**

### Why Inactivity?

| Reason | Explanation |
|--------|-------------|
| Compounding needs time | Frequent switching interrupts compounding |
| Transaction costs | Every trade has friction (tax, commission, spread) |
| Behavioral errors | More activity → more mistakes |
| Information illusion | More information ≠ better decisions |

### Punctuated Equilibrium Investing

From Eldredge & Gould's evolutionary theory: species remain stable most of the time, with sudden bursts of change.

Invest the same way:
- **95% of the time: Do nothing** — Hold quality companies, ignore noise
- **5% of the time: Act decisively** — Buy only at extreme undervaluation or sell when thesis breaks

### When to Sell (Rare Occasions Only)

| Sell Condition | Explanation |
|----------------|-------------|
| Moat permanently impaired | Irreversible competitive shift |
| Management integrity breach | Once trust is broken, no second chance |
| Original thesis proven wrong | You were wrong; admit it and exit |
| **Do NOT sell for short-term earnings miss** | Good companies have bad quarters |
| **Do NOT sell because price dropped** | Price ≠ Value |

---

## Behavioral Bias Defense (Don't Be Pavlovian)

**"In investing, as in life, reflexes can be dangerous."**

| Bias | Countermeasure |
|------|----------------|
| Panic selling | Pre-set rules: sell only when thesis breaks |
| Chasing momentum | Act contrarian: be greedy when others are fearful |
| Anchoring | Regularly reassess; don't anchor to purchase price |
| Herding | Think independently; actively seek opposing views |
| Overconfidence | Keep a decision journal; review post-hoc |

## Decision Flowchart

```
Investment Decision Process:

[1] Kill List check → Any trigger? → REJECT
                                   ↓ Pass
[2] ROCE consistently >15%? (5+ years) → No? → REJECT
                                        ↓ Yes
[3] Moat source clear and sustainable?   → Unclear? → REJECT
                                        ↓ Yes
[4] Management integrity + competence?   → Doubt? → REJECT
                                        ↓ Yes
[5] Valuation reasonable? (not bubble)   → Too expensive? → WATCHLIST, wait
                                        ↓ Fair
[6] BUY. Then be very lazy.
```

---

## One-Line Summary

> **Avoid bad companies at all costs. After buying good ones, avoid selling at all costs.**
